# Scope and Open Decisions

[Plan index](PLAN.md)

## Proposed first version

Build a common reading-to-practice flow for everyday Korean, supported by themed vocabulary. Proposed initial examples should cover a café or restaurant menu, a sign or everyday notice, and a personal media example such as a reel caption. Include curated visual scenes and imported photos, screenshots, or pasted text, with selectable words, targeted hints, typed practice, flashcard review, and verb help. Keep each example small enough to deliver the complete flow; exact content counts remain open.

The first version should let a learner:

- Choose a relevant theme and study a small vocabulary collection.
- Explore a readable Korean scene and select individual words for progressive hints.
- Import a photo, screenshot, or pasted Korean from everyday life or media, check its text, and save selected items for practice.
- Type a short response in Hangul using the vocabulary.
- Save and repeatedly review difficult words.
- Practice verb meanings and selected conjugations independently.

Proposed later additions include synchronized video practice, direct reel-link import where supported, more themes, expanded grammar and speech levels, AI conversation, speech scoring, and optional Anki compatibility. Explain encountered narrative verb forms on demand in the first version even if they fall outside the initial drill sequence.

## Questions for the next iteration

1. Is the tool mainly for personal use, or intended for other learners too?
2. What is the starting Korean level, including Hangul reading and typing?
3. Should real-world scenes support independent reading, read-aloud audio, or both?
4. Which three themes would be most useful first?
5. What devices should it work on, and is offline use important?
6. How much guidance should writing feedback provide before revealing a correction?
7. Which word-level hints are most useful: meaning clues, grammatical breakdowns, pronunciation, or a choice of all three?
8. When following reels, is the main difficulty reading on-screen text, understanding speech, keeping up with the pace, or a combination?

Resolve the learner level next, then design one complete example collection with typed writing and word-level help to make the next iteration concrete. Platform and implementation choices can follow that experience.

Check the proposed experience against both [everyday reading](examples/EVERYDAY_READING.md) and the [shared reel example](examples/SHARED_REEL.md) before narrowing the first version.
