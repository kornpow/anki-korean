# Themed Vocabulary

[Plan index](../PLAN.md)

## Learning structure

Organize broad themes into small collections built around a situation. Each collection should combine nouns, verbs, adjectives, and useful phrases.

| Theme | Example collections | Reading material | Writing task |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Food and drink | Café orders, restaurant requests, cooking | Menus, specials boards | Write an order or dietary request |
| Getting around | Subway travel, directions, taxis | Station signs, maps | Write directions |
| Shopping | Groceries, prices, product questions | Labels, price tags, receipts | Write a shopping list or question |
| Everyday life | Morning routines, home, opening hours | Notices, recycling signs | Write about your routine or a reminder |
| People and interests | Introductions, making plans, gaming, music | Messages, event posters | Write an invitation or reply |

Proposed collection flow:

1. Learn 8–12 related words with Hangul, audio, and examples.
2. Read them in a realistic scene.
3. Practice recall and short sentence construction.
4. Write something personally relevant.
5. Review difficult vocabulary and verb forms over time.

Allow direct access to reading, writing, flashcards, and verb practice without requiring completion of this sequence.

## Vocabulary entries

Each entry should include Hangul, meaning, pronunciation audio, an example sentence with translation, and its themes. Verbs should include the dictionary form and common forms used in examples. Add brief usage or politeness notes where helpful.

Example:

- Word: 커피 — coffee.
- Example: 커피 한 잔 주세요. — One coffee, please.
- Collection: Ordering at a café.

Proposed supports include optional romanization, a lightweight Hangul introduction, and personal collections. Their priority depends on the learner's current level.
