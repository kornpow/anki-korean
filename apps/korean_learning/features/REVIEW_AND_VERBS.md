# Flashcards and Verb Practice

[Plan index](../PLAN.md)

## Flashcard review

Provide a separate review area that draws words and phrases from lessons, reading scenes, personal collections, and writing corrections.

| Mode | Purpose |
| --- | --- |
| Due today | Review items on a spaced schedule based on recall |
| Difficult words | Focus on repeatedly missed or manually marked items |
| By theme | Prepare for a specific interest or situation |
| Writing recall | Type the Korean answer before revealing it |

Cards should retain their original context. For a word saved from a menu, the answer could show the menu snippet, audio, meaning, and an example sentence.

Distinguish forgetting a word from misspelling it so later practice can address the actual difficulty. Allow short, repeatable sessions focused on troublesome words.

Open decisions include the scheduling approach, answer grading, session controls, and whether Anki compatibility is useful.

## Verb workshop

Track remembering a verb's meaning separately from producing its conjugated forms. A learner may know a dictionary form while struggling with a particular tense or speech level.

Example practice using 먹다:

| Skill | Prompt | Answer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | What does 먹다 mean? | To eat |
| Vocabulary recall | Write the dictionary form for “to eat” | 먹다 |
| Present form | Use the everyday polite present form | 먹어요 |
| Past form | Use the everyday polite past form | 먹었어요 |
| Sentence writing | Write “I ate bread” | 빵을 먹었어요. |

Proposed starting point: common verbs in everyday polite 해요체, followed gradually by more tenses, irregular patterns, and other speech levels. The exact progression should reflect the learner's level.

A practice session could show a short explanation and worked example, ask for several related conjugations, mix in older verbs, and finish with a personal sentence.

Reveal hints in stages: identify the stem, explain the applicable rule, then show the answer. Return missed forms in later sessions, including sentence contexts. Prompts must specify the target tense and speech level; sentence grading should allow valid alternative answers.
