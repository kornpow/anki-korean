# Learning From a Shared Reel

[Plan index](../PLAN.md)

One concrete example: the learner's wife frequently finds Korean reels that the learner cannot easily follow. This illustrates personal media alongside equally important [everyday reading situations](EVERYDAY_READING.md), such as menus, signs, and labels. The shared reading and practice features serve all of these sources.

The supplied example is a photo of a phone showing a reel. Its headline reads:

대장이 사라진 그날 이후 모든 것이 달라졌다.

This provides an example of narrative text with attached particles and verb forms beyond the everyday polite forms used in beginner drills. The image supplies visible text only; it does not establish the reel's audio, timing, or full story.

Proposed screenshot flow:

1. Import a screenshot or photo, with pasted Korean as another entry route.
2. Rotate or crop as needed and extract the visible text. Keep the source image beside editable text and flag uncertain readings for correction.
3. Read at your own pace with translation hidden. Select one word or form for targeted help.
4. Request pronunciation, a clue, a dictionary form, or a grammatical explanation independently. Reveal the selected meaning or full phrase only when requested.
5. Save selected words and forms with their source sentence into review and relevant themes. Do not automatically turn every unfamiliar word into homework.
6. Optionally type a short reaction or reuse one expression in a personal sentence.
7. Return to the original caption later and check comprehension with less help.

For this headline, selecting 사라진 could offer help connecting it to 사라지다 and explaining its role before 그날. Selecting 달라졌다 could connect it to 달라지다 and its past-tense form. These explanations should be available on demand without revealing the whole caption's meaning.

Let a quick “help me follow this” session end after reading. Saving practice and writing a response are optional, so understanding a shared reel does not require completing a lesson.

Proposed later video support: pause, slow playback, repeat a short segment, and follow selectable captions synchronized with the clip. Distinguish on-screen writing from spoken dialogue and distinguish synthesized caption audio from the original speaker. Direct reel-link import depends on access and platform capabilities and is not assumed for the first version.

Product success example: understand a newly shared caption with fewer hints, then recognize or use one of its expressions in another context.
